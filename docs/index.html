Why it exists

We got fed up.

  • Ads between songs
  • Apps that decide what you hear
  • Feeds that play you more of the same
  • Accounts that follow you everywhere

Pocketful is a personal, non-commercial project by a small team of music lovers who had enough of big tech. So we made the music app we wanted: one that helps you find music, then leaves you alone.

So how can it be free?

  • Nothing runs on servers of ours. Everything runs on your own computer and phone.
  • There's no business model. Not a free tier, not a trial. Just free.
  • Nothing to sell. Not even you. No ads, no account, nothing collected. We don't even know who you are.

Bring your playlists

Paste a playlist's link, or bring your whole library at once from the copy of your data that most music apps let you ask for. On a phone, just share to Pocketful.

Keep it yours

Your CDs, files and playlists in a tidy library with covers, kept on your own disk. Pair your phone and computer over your own Wi-Fi, or send music to USB drives and players.

Love it? Support the artist. Every artist in Pocketful has a Support button: buy their music, see them live.

Safe and private

No viruses. Nothing hidden. And you can check.

How to install

On Windows

  1. Get Pocketful-<version>-win64.zip.
  2. Unzip it anywhere, and open Pocketful.exe once the unzipping has finished. Nothing to install. If a message about a missing file appears the first time, open it again: your antivirus was still checking the new files.
  3. If Windows says "Windows protected your PC", click More info, then Run anyway. It says this about apps that aren't signed with a paid certificate.

On Android

  1. On the phone, get Pocketful-android-<version>.apk.
  2. Open it, and allow your browser to install apps when Android asks.
  3. To bring your computer's library across, open Bring in → Your computer, if you like.
Check your download

Each release lists the SHA-256 fingerprint of every file in SHA256SUMS.txt. If your file's fingerprint is the same as the one listed, it is exactly the file that was published.

On Windows, in a Command Prompt in the folder of the download:

certutil -hashfile Pocketful-<version>-win64.zip SHA256

On Android, the easiest way is to check the .apk on a computer the same way. After the first install, Android itself refuses any update that isn't signed with the same key as the app you have, and the app checks its own updates (below).

To scan it as well, upload it to VirusTotal, which checks it with about 70 antivirus engines. If one or two little-known engines flag it, Is it safe? explains why.

SHA256SUMS.txt.sig is the project's signature of that list, made with a key that is kept offline and never goes online. The apps check it before installing anything. The public half of the key, to check it yourself, is b9daab648cf354a7838b8e92d575fc871c949cfa9fa5e5f2c6f9d6cb20b993b9. More in Is it safe?

How updates work
  • Once a day, Pocketful looks at the latest release on the project's releases page. You can turn this off in Settings → Updates.
  • A new version is installed only if its files match SHA256SUMS.txt and that list carries the project's signature. Anything else is refused, so not even someone who took over the project's GitHub account could push an update to you.
  • About once an hour it also reads the project's signed notice file, for news such as a version with a problem.
  • These are plain requests: no account, no ID, nothing about you or your device is sent. Like any website, GitHub can see your IP address.
